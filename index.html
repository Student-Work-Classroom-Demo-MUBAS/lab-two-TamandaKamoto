Bingu wa Mutharika

Economist · 3rd President of Malawi (1934 – 2012)

Bingu wa Mutharika, who served as President of Malawi from 2004 until his death in 2012.

Bingu wa Mutharika?

Bingu wa Mutharika was born Brightson Webster Ryson Thom on 24 February 1934 in Thyolo, in what was then the British protectorate of Nyasaland. He trained as an economist, earning degrees in India and the United States, and went on to build a long career in development economics, including years spent working for the World Bank and other international organisations across Southern and Eastern Africa. That technical background in economics would come to define the centrepiece of his later political career.

Mutharika returned to Malawian politics in the late 1990s and served as Minister of Economic Planning and Development under President Bakili Muluzi. In 2004 he was elected the third President of Malawi, and the following year he broke away to found his own party, the Democratic Progressive Party. His signature policy was the Farm Input Subsidy Programme, launched in 2005, which supplied smallholder farmers with subsidised fertiliser and seed. Malawi's maize harvest more than doubled within a few years, turning a food-deficit country into a net exporter and drawing international attention to the programme as a model for tackling hunger.

Re-elected in 2009, Mutharika also served a term as Chairperson of the African Union from 2010 to 2011. His second term, however, was marked by growing economic strain, fuel shortages, and rising tension with civil society and international donors over governance and human rights concerns. He died suddenly of cardiac arrest on 5 April 2012, while still in office, and was succeeded by his vice president, Joyce Banda. He remains one of the most consequential — and most debated — figures in Malawi's post-independence history.

"Democracy, governance and human rights cannot be achieved if the people are hungry."
— Bingu wa Mutharika, addressing world leaders at a food security conference

Key Life Events

  1. 1934 — Born Brightson Webster Ryson Thom in Thyolo, Nyasaland.
  2. 1960s–1970s — Studies economics in India and the United States; begins a career in development economics.
  3. 1978–1990s — Works for the World Bank and regional economic organisations across Africa.
  4. 2002 — Appointed Minister of Economic Planning and Development under President Bakili Muluzi.
  5. 2004 — Elected 3rd President of Malawi.
  6. 2005 — Founds the Democratic Progressive Party; launches the Farm Input Subsidy Programme.
  7. 2009 — Re-elected President of Malawi in a landslide victory.
  8. 2010–2011 — Serves as Chairperson of the African Union.
  9. 5 April 2012 — Dies in office in Lilongwe; succeeded by Vice President Joyce Banda.
